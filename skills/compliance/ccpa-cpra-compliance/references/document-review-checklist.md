# Document review checklist

Use this checklist for privacy policies, notices at collection, opt-out and limit pages, financial incentive notices, service provider, contractor and third-party contracts, consumer request procedures, risk assessments, ADMT pre-use notices, and papers or specifications that describe processing.

Check two things for each document: that it contains what the CCPA requires, and that it matches what the system actually does. A policy that says "we do not sell or share" while ad pixels fire is the usual finding. When both a document and code are in scope, cross-check them against the step 2 inventory.

## Privacy policy (§1798.130(a)(5); Regs. §7011)

- [ ] Categories of personal information collected in the past 12 months, their sources, and the business or commercial purpose for each
- [ ] Categories sold or shared, the categories of third parties, and the purpose; or a statement that none are
- [ ] Categories disclosed for a business purpose and the categories of recipients
- [ ] Whether the business sells or shares personal information of consumers under 16
- [ ] Whether sensitive personal information is used or disclosed beyond the Regs. §7027(m) purposes
- [ ] Retention period per category, or the criteria for it
- [ ] Rights: know, delete, correct, opt out of sale and sharing, limit, ADMT opt-out and access where applicable, non-retaliation
- [ ] How to submit requests, how verification works, how authorized agents submit, and how opt-out preference signals are processed
- [ ] Request metrics, for businesses handling 10,000,000+ consumers (Regs. §7102)
- [ ] Contact details and the date last updated, within the past 12 months

## Notice at collection (§1798.100(a)-(b); Regs. §7012)

- [ ] Given at or before collection, on every collection point (forms, apps, offline, phone)
- [ ] Categories collected, including sensitive ones, and the purposes for each
- [ ] Whether each category is sold or shared, with the link to the opt-out notice
- [ ] Retention period per category
- [ ] Link to the privacy policy; an online link lands directly on the relevant section
- [ ] Third parties that control collection on the business's site (ad networks) are named or covered (Regs. §7012(g))

## Opt-out, limit and financial incentive notices (§1798.120, .121, .125, .135; Regs. §7013-7016)

- [ ] "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" and "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" links, or the single alternative opt-out link (Regs. §7015), in the website header or footer and in the app
- [ ] The opt-out page explains the right and offers an interactive form, with no account required
- [ ] Financial incentive notice: material terms, categories of data, how to opt in and withdraw, and a good-faith estimate of the data's value with the method used

## Service provider and contractor contracts (§1798.100(d), .140(j), (ag); Regs. §7051)

- [ ] No selling or sharing of the personal information
- [ ] The specific business purposes, described specifically rather than by reference to the whole contract
- [ ] No retention, use or disclosure outside those purposes or outside the direct business relationship, including combining with other data
- [ ] Compliance with the CCPA and the same level of privacy protection, including reasonable security
- [ ] The business's right to take reasonable steps to ensure compliant use, and to stop and remediate unauthorized use
- [ ] Notice when the provider can no longer meet its obligations
- [ ] Cooperation with consumer requests, cybersecurity audits, risk assessments and ADMT obligations
- [ ] Equivalent contracts with subcontractors (Regs. §7051(b)); contractors also certify they understand the restrictions (§1798.140(j))

Third parties that receive sold or shared data need a contract with the Regs. §7053 terms: limited purposes, CCPA compliance and the same protection, business rights to ensure and remediate, and notice of inability to comply.

## Consumer request procedure (§1798.105-.130; Regs. §7020-7028, 7060-7063, 7101)

- [ ] Two or more methods for know, delete and correct requests, including a toll-free number (email alone for online-only businesses)
- [ ] Receipt confirmed within 10 business days; response within 45 calendar days, one 45-day extension with notice
- [ ] Opt-out and limit requests honored within 15 business days, without verification; third parties told of opt-outs
- [ ] Verification proportionate to risk; denials explained; authorized agent handling
- [ ] Deletion and correction passed to service providers, contractors and third parties
- [ ] Records of requests and responses kept 24 months; staff trained

## Risk assessment (Regs. §7150-7157)

- [ ] Triggered activity named: selling or sharing, sensitive data, ADMT for a significant decision, workplace or sensitive-location inferences, or training ADMT or identification technology
- [ ] Purpose, categories of personal information, operational elements (collection, retention, recipients, ADMT logic)
- [ ] Benefits, negative impacts to consumers' privacy, and safeguards for each impact
- [ ] Whether the business will proceed, the stakeholders involved, approver and date
- [ ] Conducted before the processing starts; reviewed every three years and within 45 days of a material change; retained per Regs. §7155(c)
- [ ] Submission to CalPrivacy planned for the Regs. §7157 deadline

## ADMT pre-use notice (Regs. §7220)

- [ ] The specific purpose, not "to make a significant decision"
- [ ] How to opt out, or how to appeal to a human reviewer where the exception applies
- [ ] How to request access to information about the ADMT's logic and output
- [ ] Given at or before collection, or before reuse for the ADMT purpose

## Papers and other documents

For specifications, proposals, research papers and policies that describe processing: build the inventory from the text, then check purpose and minimization, retention, sale and sharing paths, sensitive data, minors, vendor roles, and whether a risk assessment or cybersecurity audit is triggered. Record each silence in the document as an evidence gap.
