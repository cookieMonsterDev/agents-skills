# Document review checklist

Use this checklist for privacy notices, consent and cookie texts, data processing agreements, records of processing, DPIAs, breach and data subject request procedures, policies, and academic or business papers that describe processing.

Check two things for each document: that it contains what the GDPR requires, and that it matches what the system actually does. A complete notice that describes processing the code does not perform, or omits processing it does, is a transparency finding. When both a document and code are in scope, cross-check them against the step 2 inventory.

## Privacy notice (Art. 12-14)

Art. 13 applies when data is collected from the person; Art. 14 when it comes from elsewhere. Both require:

- [ ] Controller identity and contact details; EU representative where applicable (Art. 27)
- [ ] DPO contact details, where a DPO exists
- [ ] Purposes of processing and the lawful basis for each
- [ ] The legitimate interests pursued, when relying on Art. 6(1)(f)
- [ ] Recipients or categories of recipients
- [ ] Transfers outside the EEA and the safeguard relied on, with how to get a copy
- [ ] Retention period, or the criteria used to set it
- [ ] Rights: access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability
- [ ] Right to withdraw consent at any time, where consent is the basis
- [ ] Right to complain to a supervisory authority
- [ ] Whether providing data is a statutory or contractual requirement, and the consequences of not providing it (Art. 13 only)
- [ ] Automated decision-making, including profiling: its logic, significance and envisaged consequences
- [ ] Categories of personal data and their source, including public sources (Art. 14 only)

Also check: plain and concise language, layered structure for long notices, specific purposes ("to improve our services" alone is too vague), timing (at collection for Art. 13; within a month, or at first contact or disclosure, for Art. 14).

## Consent and cookie texts (Art. 4(11), 7, 8; ePrivacy Art. 5(3))

- [ ] Each purpose can be accepted or refused separately, apart from the terms of service
- [ ] Consent is an affirmative act: an unticked box or explicit button, separate from scrolling or browsing
- [ ] Reject is as prominent and easy as accept on the first layer, in a neutral design (EDPB Guidelines 03/2022)
- [ ] Names the controller and any third parties relying on the consent
- [ ] Explains how to withdraw, and withdrawal is as easy as giving consent
- [ ] Cookie list states purpose, provider and duration, and only strictly necessary cookies are exempt from consent
- [ ] Access to the service stays available when unnecessary processing is refused; assess cookie walls against national authority guidance

## Data processing agreement (Art. 28)

Art. 28(3) requires the processor to:

- [ ] Process only on documented instructions, including on transfers
- [ ] Ensure people authorized to process the data are bound by confidentiality
- [ ] Take all Art. 32 security measures
- [ ] Engage sub-processors only with prior authorization, with equivalent obligations flowed down and objection rights for general authorization
- [ ] Assist the controller with data subject requests
- [ ] Assist with Art. 32-36 obligations: security, breach notification, DPIA, prior consultation
- [ ] Delete or return all personal data at the end of services, as the controller chooses
- [ ] Provide information demonstrating compliance and allow audits and inspections

Also check: subject matter, duration, nature and purpose, data types and data subject categories are described; breach notice to the controller is without undue delay with a concrete deadline; transfer clauses (SCC module) match the actual data flow; liability terms leave the obligations intact.

## Records of processing (Art. 30)

- [ ] Controller and DPO names and contact details
- [ ] Purposes
- [ ] Categories of data subjects and personal data
- [ ] Categories of recipients, including in third countries
- [ ] Transfers and their safeguards
- [ ] Envisaged erasure time limits
- [ ] General description of security measures

Processors keep their own record under Art. 30(2). Cross-check the record against the inventory; missing activities are the usual finding.

## DPIA (Art. 35)

- [ ] Systematic description of processing and purposes
- [ ] Necessity and proportionality assessment
- [ ] Risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects, not only to the organization
- [ ] Measures that address each risk, and the residual risk
- [ ] DPO advice recorded, and data subjects' views sought where appropriate
- [ ] Prior consultation (Art. 36) when residual high risk remains
- [ ] A review date or trigger

## Breach procedure (Art. 33, 34)

- [ ] Defines what counts as a breach (confidentiality, integrity and availability)
- [ ] Clock starts at awareness; supervisory authority notified within 72 hours unless the breach is unlikely to result in risk
- [ ] Notification content per Art. 33(3), allowing information in phases
- [ ] Criteria and template for informing data subjects when high risk (Art. 34)
- [ ] Processor-to-controller notification path
- [ ] Breach register kept for all breaches, including unreported ones (Art. 33(5))

## Data subject request procedure (Art. 12, 15-22)

- [ ] Intake through any reasonable channel, with proportionate identity verification
- [ ] One-month deadline, extension by up to two further months with notice and reasons
- [ ] Free of charge, except manifestly unfounded or excessive requests
- [ ] Access response includes the copy of data and the Art. 15(1) information
- [ ] Erasure and restriction propagate to recipients (Art. 19)
- [ ] Refusals give reasons and mention the right to complain and seek a judicial remedy

## Papers and other documents

For research papers, proposals, specifications and policies that describe processing: build the inventory from the text, then check lawful basis, the research exemptions and safeguards (Art. 89 and member-state law), minimisation and pseudonymisation, consent or information given to participants, retention, sharing and transfers, and whether a DPIA is needed. Record each silence in the document as an evidence gap.
