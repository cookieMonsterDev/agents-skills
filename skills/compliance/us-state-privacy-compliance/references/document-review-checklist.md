# Document review checklist

Use this checklist for privacy notices, consent texts, request and appeal procedures, processor contracts, data protection assessments, and papers or specifications that describe processing.

Check two things for each document: that it contains what each in-scope state requires, and that it matches what the system actually does. A notice that says "we do not sell your data" while ad pixels fire is the usual finding. When both a document and code are in scope, cross-check them against the step 2 inventory.

## Privacy notice

The shared model requires:

- [ ] Categories of personal data processed, including sensitive data
- [ ] Purposes for each category
- [ ] How to exercise each right and how to appeal a refused request
- [ ] Categories of personal data shared with third parties, and the categories of those third parties
- [ ] A contact method, such as an email address or online form
- [ ] Sale and targeted advertising disclosed clearly and conspicuously, with how to opt out
- [ ] Profiling opt-out, where the controller profiles with legal or similarly significant effects

Common state additions: the right to a list of specific third parties (Oregon, Minnesota) or of their categories (Delaware), with how to request it; whether data is used to train large language models (Vermont); a dated notice and notice of material changes (Colorado Rules); the UOOM honored. Check each in-scope state's notice section against the live statute.

## Consent texts

- [ ] Freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, by a clear affirmative act
- [ ] Separate from terms of service; general acceptance, hovering or closing a banner is not consent
- [ ] Withdrawal as easy as giving consent, with processing stopped promptly after it
- [ ] Colorado: consent refreshed at the intervals the CPA Rules set, and specific disclosures for sensitive data

## Request and appeal procedure

- [ ] Secure, reliable methods described in the notice; no new account required to submit
- [ ] Response within 45 days, one 45-day extension with notice and reasons
- [ ] Free at least once in 12 months; fees or refusals only for manifestly unfounded, excessive or repetitive requests
- [ ] Authentication commercially reasonable; opt-out requests honored without it
- [ ] Appeal: conspicuous process, decision within the state's deadline (commonly 60 days), and a way to contact the attorney general if denied
- [ ] Authorized agents accepted for opt-outs where a state requires it

## Processor contract

- [ ] Instructions for processing, nature and purpose, types of data, duration, and the parties' rights and obligations
- [ ] Confidentiality duty for each person processing the data
- [ ] Deletion or return at the end of services, unless law requires retention
- [ ] Information needed to demonstrate compliance made available to the controller
- [ ] Assessments or audits allowed, or an independent assessor's report provided
- [ ] Subcontractors engaged under a written contract with the same obligations, after the controller has an opportunity to object
- [ ] Assistance with requests, security, breach notification and data protection assessments

Delaware's amended law (from 2027-01-01) also requires contracts for disclosures to third parties; check the live text.

## Data protection assessment

- [ ] Triggered activity named: targeted advertising, sale, sensitive data, or profiling with a risk of unfair or deceptive treatment, financial, physical or reputational injury, intrusion, or other substantial injury
- [ ] Benefits to the controller, consumer, other stakeholders and the public weighed against risks to consumers' rights
- [ ] Safeguards that reduce the risks, and the context: de-identified data, consumer expectations, relationship
- [ ] Colorado: the CPA Rules' content list, stakeholders, and timing (before processing; review when processing changes)
- [ ] Retained and producible to the attorney general on request; dates of the triggering processing covered by the law's effective-date rule

## Papers and other documents

For specifications, proposals, research papers and policies that describe processing: build the inventory from the text, then check purpose and minimization, retention, targeted advertising, sale and profiling paths, sensitive data and minors, vendor roles, and whether a data protection assessment is triggered. Record each silence in the document as an evidence gap.
