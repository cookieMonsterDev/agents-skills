# Mature income payers, steady compounders and high growers

Use this playbook when distributions, durable earning power or repeatable expansion drive value. Classification is a researched hypothesis, not a threshold on one year's revenue growth. Hybrid businesses can use more than one branch.

## Mature income payer: can distributions persist?

Gather dividend history, earnings, CFO/capex, debt terms, reinvestment needs and payout policy. Separate ordinary and special dividends. Reconcile cash distributions including preferred holders to the common-share thesis. Inspect whether payouts rely on asset sales, borrowing or underinvestment.

Use `analyze_report.py` for annual cash/earnings evidence. Calculate earnings and cash payout coverage only after verifying the distribution scope and period. Use the `stress` model for a distribution-cut/downturn case when liquidity is consequential; an `earnings` scenario excludes interim dividends, so it is insufficient for a dividend-led value conclusion. A dividend-discount model requires a separately documented payout forecast and cost of equity.

Complete with a distribution sustainability conclusion, the assumptions supporting it, and observable cut-risk triggers. A high current yield or long payment record alone is insufficient.

## Steady compounder: is quality supported by recurring economics?

Gather multiple years of segment sales, margins, cash generation, competitive position and recession sensitivity. Inspect acquisitions and capital returns: was EPS growth driven by operating earnings or share shrinkage? Can retained capital earn attractive returns? Verify customer/product concentration and evidence for pricing power; stable aggregate margins can conceal a deteriorating segment.

Use annual evidence and a comparable interim update. Build a reported-to-normalized earnings bridge and optional `earnings` or `dcf` cases. Sensitize slower organic growth, margin pressure, capital requirements and multiple compression. Explain why chosen peers are economically comparable before using multiples.

Complete with separate assessments of operating quality and valuation, evidence for durability, and the operating deterioration that would invalidate the thesis.

## High grower: what makes expansion repeatable?

Gather the growth product's share of consolidated results, organic/acquired growth, unit/cohort economics when disclosed, retention, competition, capacity, reinvestment and funding. Test whether sales expansion converts to earnings and cash per share. Explain stock compensation and potential dilution. A large market estimate alone does not establish profitable growth.

Use `analyze_report.py` for comparable growth/margins and cash conversion. Use `earnings` only with defensible positive normalized EPS; for loss-makers, use a supported operating forecast to profitability or finish without an earnings multiple. Use `dcf` only after constructing FCFF. Include a slowdown case with weaker margins, greater reinvestment and lower terminal valuation; expose reliance on terminal value.

Rules of thumb comparing P/E with growth, or preferring a particular growth band, are heuristics, not valuation models. If requested, compute PEG as P/E divided by growth in **percentage points** (15% becomes 15), with positive earnings/growth and an explicitly named forecast horizon. Distinguish forward estimates from historical growth; PEG is a cross-check, not an intrinsic value model. A growth-plus-yield heuristic needs matched units and sustainable distributions.

Complete with the expansion mechanism, economic constraints, per-share outcome, downside funding needs and milestones that distinguish an achieved result from management's ambition.

## Shared thesis drill

Write a brief thesis explaining how this business creates cash for shareholders, why that can persist, the valuation assumptions required and the most credible failure path. Cite the evidence for each causal claim. Add the overlay from [risk-and-opportunity-cost.md](risk-and-opportunity-cost.md).
